## Question 1

```text
Round: Fungi icon: round 4, the pane with a hint of life
Question: Which direction should we refine?
Description (Markdown source):
Same restraint as round 3 (a pane, graphite or paper fields, one accent), plus **one** biological trait each, instead of a literal mushroom.

- **Membrane**: A crisp window frame holding a living cell: mint cytoplasm, a dark nucleus, a soft glow. *The pane is alive inside.* The most overtly biological.
- **Division**: Two ink panes joined by a pinched neck, mid-division, each with a moss nucleus. *The agent buds a new pane for the person.* Flat, on paper.
- **Reach**: A bone-white pane that stretches a pseudopod toward a glowing point. It also reads as a speech bubble: *the interface reaches out to communicate.*
- **Tissue**: A pane made of rounded cells, one of them lit mint. *Many interfaces in one engine; one is active now.*
- **Hyphae**: Round 3's Frame: two rigid corners, now joined across the gaps by fine filament with small forks. *Part built, part grown.* Below 24px it falls back to the plain frame.
- **Seed head**: A pane holding a phyllotaxis pattern (the golden-angle spiral of seed heads), bright at the core and fading out. *Order that grows.* The quietest.

Each card shows the full icon, then 40 / 24 / 16px on light, plus a circle crop and 24 / 16px on dark. The accent (mint/moss) and fields are placeholders. Say in Notes if a shape is right but its treatment isn't, or if the life hint should be stronger or fainter.
Recommendation: Division. It's the best balance: plain geometry that still reads as unmistakably alive. It holds up at 16px and in a circle, and the story fits: the agent buds a pane for the person. Runner-up is Reach, if communication should lead.
```
